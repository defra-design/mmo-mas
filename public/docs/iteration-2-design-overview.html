Iteration 2 – design overview

Iteration 2 develops the receive and assess journey from reviewing an application into recording assessment decisions and preparing the case for consultation. It adds individual marine plan policy assessments, consultation preparation, public register decisions, public notice and evidence review, and a task to start consultation. It also explores how case officers reject an application or arrange a transfer to MCMS.

This overview describes the current prototype, including the latest additions to Iteration 2. It builds on the Iteration 1 design overview.

Different ways of displaying tasks and marine plan policy assessments

Iteration 2 explores four ways of organising the case officer’s work. Each uses the same individual policy assessment form; the difference is where the tasks and policy assessments sit, and how the case officer moves between reviewing the application and assessing it.

  • MLA/2026/10015 – dedicated Tasks tab

    The Tasks tab is the first thing the case officer sees when entering the case. It shows the full-width Tasks grid, with the marine plan policy assessment grid underneath. All assessment work is brought together in one place, with no Tasks side panel.

    The separate Marine plan policies tab remains available for reviewing the applicant’s policy answers, grouped by sector. The case officer can review related answers there and open individual assessment records from the Tasks tab.

  • MLA/2026/10014 – persistent Tasks panel and assessments on the MPP tab

    The Tasks panel stays visible on the right while the case officer moves between tabs. The Marine plan policies tab contains the full-width assessment grid, replacing the separate applicant-answer view. Opening a policy brings its applicant information and the case officer’s assessment together on one form. Save and close returns to the MPP tab to choose the next policy.

  • MLA/2026/10013 – assessments below Case summary and a persistent Tasks panel

    The Tasks panel stays visible on the right. The full-width marine plan policy assessment grid sits below the Case summary, allowing the case officer to open each policy to review the applicant’s answer and record a decision. The separate Marine plan policies tab retains the read-only applicant answers.

  • MLA/2026/10012 – tasks and policy assessments in the side panel

    The persistent right-hand panel contains the Tasks list followed by a second list of individual marine plan policy assessments. Policies appear in a compact, paginated list rather than a full-width grid. The Marine plan policies tab retains the read-only applicant answers.

Detailed exploration: ML-1329 – marine plan policy assessments and layout variants.

Changes to applicant information

  • Application details

    The applicant service changed its “Projects” page to “Submissions”. To keep the language consistent, Application details, Other permissions and WFD now refer to the application and proposed works in place of projects. Application details uses “Application name” and “Proposed works summary”, and displays preferred licence dates at month and year level.

  • Sites and activities

    MLA/2026/10002 adds an example with multiple sites and activities, keeping each activity associated with its site. Sub-activities are listed explicitly. Case officers can also view the construction drawings applicants must submit for new construction, alterations or improvement works, with links alongside the relevant site information.

  • WFD, Other permissions and Public register

    Refines the applicant question wording, Yes/No answers and document links. Public register examples show both an applicant request to withhold information and a case where nothing has been requested for withholding.

Assessment tasks

  • Site check

    Refines the existing task with separate sections for coordinates and shape, the WFD assessment area, and optional notes. Required answers are checked on Save and close, which now unlocks the additional assessment tasks introduced in this iteration.

  • Water Framework Directive

    Adds a required “Record your considerations” field when the case officer answers Yes to “Is the WFD section complete and acceptable?”. The field is hidden and not required for a No answer. The applicant question wording and assessment-document link have also been refined.

  • Marine plan policy assessments

    There are 41 policy assessments across four groups: Cross-cutting, Economic, Environmental and Social. Each policy has its own record, showing the policy group, policy information and applicant’s consideration. The case officer records an outcome of Compliant, Non-compliant or Consultation required, with a required reason.

    The policy grid shows Policy, Group and Status, with sorting and filtering. Task status is separate from the assessment outcome: a completed assessment is Done, whichever outcome was recorded. Assessments open read-only until Site check is complete, then become To do.

  • Prepare for consultation

    After Site check is complete, the case officer selects organisations from a searchable lookup and chooses what each should receive. Application notification tells an organisation about the application without asking for a response. Request for advice reveals notes explaining what the organisation should consider.

    Selecting an organisation reveals the next blank row. At least one organisation and a consultation type for every selected organisation are required before saving. The case officer can save valid work as In progress, or tick the completion checkbox and save it as Done. In the intended service, organisations receiving requests for advice can read the notes and respond.

    More detail: ML-1573 – Prepare for consultation.

  • Public register

    Sits directly below Site check, bringing withholding decisions early enough for the applicant to receive a response and decide whether to continue. The case officer reviews the applicant’s request and assesses commercial or industrial confidentiality, national security, or both. They can agree in full, agree in part or disagree, recording a rationale and, for partial agreement or refusal, a message for the applicant.

    A personal-information check always appears, including when no withholding request was made. The case officer then follows a link to the separate redaction journey in a new tab. Required fields must be completed before saving: ticking the completion checkbox makes the task Done; leaving it unticked saves it as In progress.

    The examples also compare guidance: MLA/2026/10014 displays it openly with information icons, while MLA/2026/10015 uses collapsed “Help with…” accordions. MLA/2026/10013 shows no withholding request, so the assessment section is omitted and personal-information guidance is in an accordion.

    More detail: ML-1520 – Public register task and guidance variants.

    Redact the application

    The link opens a page on CDP, Defra’s Core Development Platform, where the application data is stored. It uses the GOV.UK Design System, presenting the application in a similar way to the applicant service.

    The page shows the submitted application and the applicant’s withholding request. The case officer edits a copy of an answer for publication while preserving the original, using a consistent redaction marker. They can withhold a site’s coordinates and map together, withhold documents, or upload redacted replacement documents. Redactions can also be changed or removed.

    A preview opens in a new tab to show the public version, with withheld information omitted. Save redactions records the work and checks that no answers remain open for editing. This prepares the application for publication; publishing happens back in MAS.

    More detail: ML-1383 – redacting answers, locations and documents.

  • Public notice

    The case officer decides whether a public notice is required. The task currently supports site notices, with its broader name allowing for other notice types in future. Selecting None requires an explanation; selecting Site notice reveals fields for a shorter, public-facing summary and the audience: marine users, community users or both.

    The applicant’s proposed works summary is shown as a read-only reference. Expandable guidance helps the case officer write the notice summary, including a worked example. The audience choice determines the suggested notice locations the applicant sees, such as harbour noticeboards or coastal access points.

    Save and close checks the required fields without a separate completion checkbox. None marks the task Done; Site notice changes it to Awaiting applicant while the applicant displays the notice and provides evidence. Before Site check is complete, the task can be opened read-only and closed without progressing it.

    More detail: ML-1381 – Public notice task.

  • Review public notice evidence and resubmissions

    When the applicant submits evidence, the Public notice task becomes Done and a separate Review public notice evidence task appears as To do. For each location, the case officer sees the display date and links to a close-up photograph and a photograph of the surroundings. They accept or reject each location separately, with required comments explaining any rejection.

    Save and close checks the decisions and comments. All accepted means Done; any rejection means Awaiting applicant. The intended applicant journey asks for replacement photographs only for rejected locations. Resubmission reuses the existing review task, showing Resubmitted - to review, with earlier evidence and decisions retained as read-only history. The accept/reject cycle repeats until the evidence is satisfactory.

    MLA/2026/10015 shows the stage before evidence arrives; MLA/2026/10014 shows the initial review; MLA/2026/10013 shows replacement photographs for Location 1, with Locations 2 and 3 already accepted.

    More detail: ML-1544 – receiving and reviewing public notice evidence.

  • Start consultation

    Appears in the task list from the outset as Cannot start yet, opening read-only until the required assessment work is complete. Once ready, the case officer confirms that consultation can start and selects Save and close. The case status changes to Consultation and the task becomes Done and read-only.

    The task explains the intended publication of the application and sending of the notifications and advice requests prepared earlier. Outstanding site-notice evidence does not prevent consultation starting once the notice has been prepared. The index includes a shortcut that loads a ready-to-start example.

    More detail: ML-1593 – Start consultation.

Case actions

  • Reject application

    A command-bar action opens a dialog for selecting the application sections with issues and writing the details the applicant needs. Both are required. Confirming changes the case status to Rejected and records the reasons, explanation, person and date in a read-only section on the Case summary.

  • Transfer to MCMS

    A two-step flow shares the work between the case officer and the Business Support Team. The case officer selects Request transfer to MCMS in the command bar and enters the required reasons. Confirming changes the case status to Transfer pending and adds a Transfer to MCMS section to Case summary, showing who requested the transfer, when and why. The command changes to Complete transfer to MCMS.

    After manually copying the case into MCMS, the Business Support Team uses that command to enter the required MCMS reference. Confirming changes the status to Transferred, adds who completed the transfer, the date and reference to the same details section, and removes the command. Both statuses also appear in the Case list, helping Business Support identify cases waiting for transfer.

    More detail: ML-1379 – Transfer to MCMS.

Task form and status changes

Task forms use clearer sections, consistent required markers and padlocks, and Save and close commands. Required-field checks show a form-level message and feedback beside the affected fields. Tasks that cannot start yet can now be inspected read-only, with a notification explaining the prerequisite.

Rejection, transfer and consultation actions update the status in both the Case list and case header.

Prototype scope

The examples illustrate different stages of the journey using prepared application and evidence data. The redaction summary describes the separate journey documented in ML-1383.

The current Start consultation gate checks the required tasks and two policy assessments (SW-CC-1 and SW-CC-3) as a prototype shortcut. The ready-to-start example fills all policy assessments. This shortcut does not define the final service rule.
